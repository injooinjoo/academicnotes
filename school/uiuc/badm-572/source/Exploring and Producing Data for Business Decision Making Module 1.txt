Important Terminology - Slide 9
Data:
facts and figures from which conclusions can be drawn
Variable:
any characteristic of an element
Data set:
the data that are collected for a particular study

17
Transcript
Learning statistic is like leaning in foreign language. You must first learn the
vocabulary. For this reason, we will start off with a number of terms for you to
learn. Data are facts and figures from which conclusions can be drawn. For
example, your income, your age, your education level, are all examples of data.
To collect data, we first have to define any particular characteristics that we are
interested in. These will be the variables. For example, we may want to know
who is interested in this course. For this study, the variables for which we need to
collect data could be the gender, age, occupation, education level, and
geographical location. Then, for each element of the study, we will collect data
items for the variables of interest, and the result will be the data set. Data set is
providing information about the subjects of the study. The subject of the study
can be anything that we want to understand better. It can be people, such as an
example I just used, or it could be events or objects.
Types of Variables (1 of 2) - Slide 10
Quantitative
These will be numerical values that represent quantity—sometime referred to as
numerical data. For example: annual salary, age.

18
Transcript
We need to assign a value to each variable. Variables that are quantitative will
always have a numerical value, which will represent quantities of some sort.
Quantitative variables also referred to as numerical variables, represent
quantities measured with a fixed or standard unit of measure. For instance, age,
income, temperature are all numbers which represent the quantity and have a
unit. Money, for example, could be in dollars, and temperature is in Fahrenheit.
Types of Variables (2 of 2) - Slide 11
Qualitative (Categorical)
These will be categorical—sometimes referred to as categorical data. For
example: gender, ethnicity, etc.
This type can be:
Both non-numeric or numeric
Nominative— no meaningful order exists. Example: gender
Ordinal— can be ordered. Example: level of satisfaction

19
Transcript
A qualitative data represents a category, so these are known as categorical data.
For example, gender is a category, and the data collected about the gender of
the study subjects will be a qualitative data. Now, this type of a data can be non-
numeric, for example, a person's gender can be simply recorded as male or
female, or you can assign a number to a category, which means they can be
numeric as well. For example, if you ask someone to rate a service from scale of
1 to 5, 1 being extremely dissatisfied, and 5 being extremely satisfied, then the
data collected from the subjects will be a qualitative data, even though it's a
numerical value. In this case, numerical value is representing the level of
satisfaction as the category, not a quantity. One distinction here is that the
numbers don't have a unit of measurement. They're just numbers. Now, this type
of the data can be nominal or ordinal. A nominative variable is a qualitative
variable for which there is no meaningful ordering or ranking of these categories.
For example, a person's gender, a person's state of residence, are all nominative
variables. On the other hand, an ordinal variable is a qualitative variable for which
there is a meaningful ordering or ranking of the categories, whether or not the
data recorded numerically or non-numerically.
Here's an example. A customer can rate their level of satisfaction as excellent,
good, average, poor, or unsatisfactory. Here, one category is higher than the next
one. Therefore, customer level of satisfaction is an ordinal variable having non-
numeric measures. On the other hand, if you substitute numbers: 4, 3, 2, 1, zero
for ratings excellent to unsatisfactory, then the customer satisfaction is an ordinal
variable having numerical measurements.

What Is In The Data Set? - Slide 14
Population: set of all elements about which we wish to draw a conclusion.
Sample: a subset of the units of a population.
Drawing of the outline of Illinois with several stick figures inside labeled
Population. Drawing of a circle with a few stick figures inside labeled Sample.

22
Transcript
If we want to collect data on every single element of interest, then we have data
based on population. For example, if you wanted to know the employment status
of every person in Illinois, then we need to speak to every single person residing
Illinois. As a matter of fact, every 10 years, the U.S. Government conducts a
Census, which will collect data on many variables for every single person living in
United States at the time that the Census is being conducted. However, doing a
study on a population is most often too expensive and too time-consuming. So,
instead, we select a subset of population to collect data from. This subset is
known as sample. When we are doing a sample study for example, political polls
that predict the outcome of an election, are performed by collecting data on a
sample of a likely voters.
Parameters and Statistics - Slide 15
A population parameter is a value based on the population.
A sample statistic is a value calculated from the sample information.
Transcript
When a summary measure that describes some aspect of data set is computed
using a population data from Census, it is called a population parameter. For
example, population mean is the average of all values in population. When the
same measurement is calculated using sample data, it is called a sample
statistic. We use sample statistics for estimating values for the population.

used throughout this course, and more importantly, in any field that statistics is
being used. So, it is important that you know what exactly they mean. We will
learn other terms as our course progresses, and your knowledge of this subject
matter continues to increase.
